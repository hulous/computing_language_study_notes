# Gary Kildall

## 🔹 1. Who He Was

- **Gary Arlen Kildall** (1942–1994) was an American computer scientist and entrepreneur from Seattle, WA.
- Earned a **B.S. in Mathematics (1967)**, an **M.S. (1968)**, and a **Ph.D. in Computer Science (1972)**, all from the University of Washington.
- Taught as a professor at the **Naval Postgraduate School** in Monterey, CA, while consulting for Intel on the side.

---

## 🔹 2. From Intel Consultant to OS Pioneer

- While consulting for Intel in the early 1970s, Kildall wrote **PL/M** (Programming Language for Microcomputers), one of the first high-level languages for microprocessors.
- To test and debug his PL/M compiler, he needed a way to store and retrieve programs on a floppy disk — so in **1974** he wrote a small control program to manage that: **CP/M** (Control Program for Microcomputers).
- CP/M turned out to be more significant than the compiler it was built to support: it was arguably the **first operating system for a microcomputer**.

---

## 🔹 3. Digital Research and CP/M's Dominance

- In 1976, Kildall and his wife **Dorothy McEwen** founded **Intergalactic Digital Research**, later shortened to **Digital Research, Inc. (DRI)**.
- Kildall generalized CP/M by isolating hardware-specific code into a separate layer called the **BIOS** (Basic Input/Output System) — a concept that let CP/M be ported across many different machines with different hardware.
- By the late 1970s, CP/M was the **de facto standard operating system** for 8-bit microcomputers, running on machines from dozens of manufacturers.
- DRI's revenue grew from roughly $100,000/month in 1978 to over **$44 million/year by 1983**.

---

## 🔹 4. The IBM Deal That Didn't Happen

- In 1980, IBM was building its first personal computer and needed an operating system. It first approached **Digital Research** about licensing CP/M.
- The popular legend says Kildall skipped the IBM meeting to go flying in his airplane — this story is **widely considered a myth**, repeated for decades but denied by Kildall's colleagues and unsupported by the historical record.
- The more accepted account: Kildall (or DRI, in his absence that day) balked at IBM's proposed terms — a one-time flat fee rather than a per-copy royalty — and the two sides failed to reach an agreement.
- IBM turned instead to the young **Microsoft**, which bought a CP/M-like operating system called **QDOS** (Quick and Dirty Operating System, written by Tim Paterson) from **Seattle Computer Products**, adapted it, and licensed it to IBM as **PC-DOS / MS-DOS**.

---

## 🔹 5. Aftermath

- DRI later argued MS-DOS was derivative of CP/M's design and command structure; IBM eventually offered CP/M-86 as an option on the original PC, priced far higher than PC-DOS, which sealed the market outcome in Microsoft's favor.
- CP/M's market share collapsed over the following years as MS-DOS became the standard for IBM-compatible PCs.
- Kildall went on to other ventures, including **KnowledgeSet** (an early CD-ROM/multimedia company) and **Prometheus Light and Sound**, and co-hosted the PBS series **Computer Chronicles** throughout the 1980s.
- He died in **1994** after a fall at a Monterey bar, at age 52.

---

## 🔹 6. Legacy

- Introduced the **BIOS** abstraction, still a foundational concept in PC architecture today.
- CP/M's command conventions (drive letters, `.COM` files, the general shape of a command-line shell) directly influenced MS-DOS.
- In 2014, the **IEEE** installed a Milestone plaque in Pacific Grove, CA, recognizing Kildall's 1974 CP/M prototype as the birth of the PC operating system.
- Often cited as the clearest example of a foundational figure in computing history who never received the recognition (or fortune) of contemporaries like Bill Gates.

---

## ✅ A CP/M Command, for Flavor

```
A>DIR
A>PIP B:=A:HELLO.ASM
A>SUBMIT COMPILE
```

CP/M's `A>` prompt (one drive letter, one program at a time) was the direct ancestor of the `C:\>` prompt that would define PCs for the next two decades.
